# Description style

This file is the whole prompt behind every description the app writes. It is a starting point -
make it yours on the **Style** tab (or edit the file directly). Your copy is called
`description_style.md` and stays on your computer; this template is the one that ships with the app.

Two ways to change it, both on the Style tab:

- type what should change in plain words ("from now on add a surf hashtag group") and accept the
  diff Claude proposes, or
- edit the text yourself and press Save.

Either way, every description written after that follows the new style, and the previous version is kept so you
can go back.

## About the shop

Replace this paragraph with your own shop in a sentence or two: your Depop handle, what you sell,
the eras or aesthetics you lean on, and how you photograph (mannequin, flat lay, model). The
descriptions lean on it for tone, so be specific.

> Example: Depop handle **yourshop**: secondhand and vintage clothing, mostly 90s and Y2K, styled
> around film and TV aesthetics. Listings are photographed on an XS-S mannequin.

## Input you give

One or more photos plus a short facts line, for example
`levis, 90s, W28, good condition, small mark on hem` or
`no brand, size M, cotton, preowned, fading at the cuffs`.

## The template

Every line below is its own line. No blank lines anywhere in the description.

```
yourshop [Era] [Brand] [item] in [colour].
[Construction details - closures, trims, silhouette, fit.]
Very [character / aesthetic / vibe].
Brand: ...
Size: [tag size] - fits [size], see measurements
Material: ...
Color: ...
Era: ...
Condition: ...
Measurements: see photos
[Your fit-reference line, e.g. "XS-S mannequin shown for fit reference."]
[Your closing line, e.g. "Bundle for a discount. [Styling items] not included."]
#specific #style #era #tags
```

Line 1 opens with your handle, then the era (only for vintage pieces), the brand, a
two-to-four-word name for the garment, and the colour. Use the words buyers actually search:
"waffle knit", "baby tee", "toggle jacket", a print name.

Line 2 is the construction: closures, trims, sleeves, neckline, pockets, hem, print, then the fit
in a few words ("Fitted through the body.", "Oversized and boxy.").

Line 3 is the vibe: the character, film or aesthetic **this** piece belongs to, matching the
hashtags below. Pick it for the piece in front of you; never reuse the vibe from the examples out
of habit.

## The fields

**Brand, Size, Material, Measurements and Condition are always present**, even when the answer is
a placeholder. Era only for vintage pieces; leave the line out otherwise.

- `Brand:` the label. Unknown and no legible tag -> `[brand?]`.
- `Size:` the tag size, then what it really fits: `S - fits S, see measurements`. For non-standard
  sizing add the conversion.
- `Material:` fibre content from the care tag when it is legible in the photos, otherwise what is
  plainly visible ("Velour", "Waffle knit cotton blend"). Nothing legible -> `[material?]`.
- `Color:` plain colour name, the same one used in line 1.
- `Era:` 90s, Y2K, 00s, vintage - only when you say so or it is unmistakable.
- `Condition:` your own wording, verbatim when you give it. Flaws are named plainly and never
  hidden; a photographed flaw is "see photos". A small flaw may be framed reassuringly
  ("barely noticeable when worn"), never concealed.
- `Measurements:` `see photos` when a tape photo is among the item's photos, otherwise the numbers
  you gave.

## Hashtags

The last line: **4-6 specific style, era or character tags**, lowercase, no spaces inside a tag.

Pick the aesthetic the piece genuinely belongs to and take its tags from the bank below. Never pad
with generic tags: no #fashion, #style, #clothing, #top, #jacket, #thrift, #ootd, #depop, #cute.
A brand tag counts as specific only when the brand is itself a searched aesthetic.

Different pieces get different tags. Do not fall back on the aesthetic used in the Examples;
two listings share the same tags only when they genuinely share an aesthetic.

**This bank is a starting point - replace it with the aesthetics you actually sell into.**

**90s minimal** - slip skirts, tie-front tops, vests, mini kilts
`#90s #90sfashion #minimalist #vintage`

**Y2K** - velour, rhinestones, metallics, low-rise
`#y2k #00s #mcbling #glam`

**Preppy / academia** - knits, cardigans, plaid, corduroy
`#preppy #darkacademia #fall #collegiate`

**Soft / romantic** - satin, lace trim, bows, ruffles
`#coquette #balletcore #cottagecore`

## Rules

- Plain, honest, no hype words, no emojis. Flaws are disclosed, never obscured.
- The facts you type win over anything seen in the photos; your condition wording is kept verbatim.
- Never invent a brand, size, material, measurement or flaw. If it is not stated and not legible on
  a tag in the photos, write a placeholder such as `[size?]` so it is easy to spot.
- Read brand, size, fibre content and care details from label and care tags when legible.
- Styling props (the mannequin's shorts, a bag, a necklace) are never part of the item.
- Keep the whole text under 900 characters (Depop allows 1000), hashtags included.
- Besides the listing text, give one short practical tip when there is something worth saying
  ("add a close-up of the care tag"); otherwise leave it empty. The tip is shown to you, never
  listed.
